# Public summary

## Research question

The study examined whether AI can become a credible Cardano adoption vertical with identifiable buyers, delivery partners, revenue or transaction pathways and measurable Cardano-side activity. It tested Cardano's position against specialised AI networks, agent-payment infrastructure, general-purpose chains and non-chain alternatives.

## Method

The research used a screening-first method. Commercial demand and buyer requirements came before technical fit. Evidence was gathered through source-dated desk research, comparative cases, Cardano activity review and professional field discussions.

The final evidence base contains:

- 75 public and technical source entries;
- 38 controlled claim records;
- 8 comparison cases;
- 12 Cardano or Cardano-adjacent activity cases;
- 12 scored use cases; and
- 33 qualifying field records across six stakeholder segments.

The stakeholder segments were institutional buyers and adopters, AI-agent builders and platforms, payments and commerce infrastructure, Cardano ecosystem operators, non-Cardano infrastructure and comparison cases, and standards, identity, security and assurance specialists.

Field evidence is reported at segment level. Respondent identities, raw notes, organisation-to-response mappings and commercially sensitive information are not published. The field sample is purposive and supports qualitative analysis, not estimates of market prevalence.

## Main findings

### Agent payments are real but the blockchain opportunity is selective

Machine payments and agentic commerce have reached production infrastructure. Public blockchains add the clearest value when independent parties need programmable settlement, conditional release, portable value or a neutral shared receipt. Existing merchant, bank, card and private authorization systems remain stronger for many consumer and closed-enterprise workflows.

### Masumi and Sokosumi are Cardano's strongest live evidence

Masumi combines agent registration, escrow-based payment, refunds and service records on Cardano. Sokosumi presents priced AI services through an operating task workflow.

The Cardano app profile showed 1,852 Masumi on-chain transactions in the 30 days captured by its 21 October 2026 snapshot. Masumi reported more than 40,000 cumulative on-chain transactions. The Sokosumi Serviceplan page displayed 4,229 tasks on 29 October 2026 while labelling the profile data 27 September 2026.

These figures establish live capability and observable activity. They do not establish unique external buyers, paid conversion, repeat use, accepted outcomes or product-market fit. The sources use different activity definitions and do not provide a common commercial denominator.

### Distribution is the main competitive weakness

Leading payment and agent-commerce approaches are open and rail-agnostic. Their adoption is pulled by wallets, facilitators, stablecoin liquidity, developer tooling and existing merchant or platform channels. Base, Solana, Tempo, Stripe and card networks have stronger visible distribution. Cardano should integrate with existing standards rather than ask builders to adopt a proprietary Cardano agent stack.

### Service assurance is the strongest product wedge

Cardano's most credible use case is the conditional purchase of machine-delivered services where non-delivery, refund or later dispute matters. Payment-linked identity and minimal receipts can strengthen that use case. They are not independently unique product categories.

Cardano's deterministic execution model and formal-methods heritage can strengthen a specified on-chain control. Those properties cannot prove an AI model's intent, the correctness of an off-chain result, regulatory compliance or the truth of recorded data.

### A broad Cardano AI vertical is not supported

The evidence does not support direct competition in decentralised compute, model or inference markets, generic AI applications or mainstream consumer checkout. Specialised networks and established payment channels have stronger supply, liquidity, product focus and distribution.

## Use-case priorities

| Priority | Use case | Final posture | Evidence still required |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Escrowed purchase of machine-delivered services | Conditional go | Two independent providers and three external buyers define a paid workflow and acceptance criteria |
| 2 | Pay-per-call API or data access with conditional protection | Priority validation | A buyer chooses conditional settlement over an existing facilitator or subscription route |
| 3 | Minimal agent-action and payment receipts | Conditional test | Two independent risk or audit owners accept a minimal schema, privacy design and dispute use |
| 4 | AI-agent marketplace on Masumi and Sokosumi | Conditional test | Separate reporting of paid, completed, refunded, disputed and repeat transactions with external-buyer confirmation |
| 5 | Payment-linked agent identity and reputation | Conditional test | Evidence that portable history changes a cross-platform counterparty decision |

Compute markets, model markets and generic AI applications remain no-go or excluded from vertical proof. Privacy-preserving settlement is an adjacent handoff because Midnight and other privacy systems require a separate interoperability and buyer analysis.

## Final recommendation

The study recommends a **conditional go** for a narrow Cardano position in assurance and conditional settlement for open AI-agent services.

Further investment should require:

1. independent buyers who define a paid use case and reason to prefer conditional settlement;
2. an integration path through existing agent, authorization, payment, wallet and stablecoin infrastructure;
3. production reporting that separates external buyers, paid work, repeat use, refunds, disputes and accepted outcomes; and
4. evidence that the Cardano-based control changes a purchase, risk or dispute outcome.

The position should move toward no-go if buyers prefer simple payment or conventional escrow, Cardano integration remains materially harder without a compensating benefit, or the same outcome can be achieved more simply without Cardano.

## Recommended next actions

- Define one narrow escrowed service workflow with two providers and at least three external buyers before funding implementation.
- Publish an open-standard integration and conformance path with a non-Cardano builder, wallet or facilitator and stablecoin route.
- Test a minimal assurance receipt with two independent risk or audit owners and document the privacy and dispute process.
- Route stablecoin distribution, privacy, identity interoperability, L2 requirements and delivery-partner development to the relevant adjacent workstreams.

Cardano stakeholders should stop claiming that transaction counts prove a strategic AI vertical, that formal methods make AI safe, or that Cardano leads machine-payment distribution. They should also avoid funding proprietary standards, broad AI campaigns or compute and model markets without new buyer evidence.

## Limitations

The 33 field records are not statistically representative. Some interactions were structured calls and others were focused conference or professional discussions. Public metrics are dynamic and often provider-reported. The study did not perform the optional deep technical audit of a named deployment, use a paid market database or run a six-month monitoring programme. Cardano association was assessed separately from Cardano dependency.

The evidence supports a strategic posture and the next validation tests. It does not support revenue forecasts, market-share estimates or a claim of proven product-market fit.
