# Recorded overview script

This script supports the contractual 20 to 45 minute recorded project overview. It follows the final readout deck and is designed for approximately 22 to 26 minutes at a measured speaking pace. It can also be used for the 24-minute presentation portion of the 30-minute live readout, leaving at least six minutes for community questions.

## Slide 1 — Project and final decision

This study asked a deliberately narrow question: where, if anywhere, does Cardano have a credible commercial position in AI-agent commerce? The answer is a conditional go, but only for a focused position in assurance and conditional settlement for open AI-agent services.

The evidence supports testing three related functions: escrowed purchase of machine-delivered services, conditional payment for API or data access, and minimal receipts that can support a cross-organisation risk or dispute decision. It does not support a general AI-chain strategy, a claim that Cardano leads machine payments, a compute or model marketplace, or a broad statement that Cardano makes AI safe.

This distinction matters. Cardano can provide useful transaction controls in selected workflows, but those controls do not validate an AI model's intent, output quality or business truth. The recommendation therefore stays tied to observable buyer problems, competing alternatives and explicit stop conditions.

## Slide 2 — Research question and scope

The research began with a commercial test rather than a technology thesis. We asked where there is an identifiable buyer, a material problem, a reachable delivery route and a reason for a Cardano-based control to change the outcome.

The work covered market and protocol evidence, current Cardano and adjacent activity, comparative cases, interviews and professional discussions, use-case scoring, buyer and audience credibility, delivery pathways and competitive alternatives. Each candidate use case had to identify the buyer, problem, required capability, Cardano relevance, strongest alternative, supporting evidence, delivery route, value path, adoption signal, confidence and recommended action.

The scope excluded product development, pilot execution, revenue forecasting, broad market sizing and the optional proposal add-ons. It also excluded advocacy. A no-go result was always valid, and several attractive-sounding categories were rejected because the evidence did not show a distinct Cardano role.

## Slide 3 — Method and evidence base

The method was screening first. Commercial demand and buyer requirements came before technical fit. A high technical score could not compensate for weak evidence, lack of buyer access or a stronger conventional alternative.

The final evidence cut contains 75 public and technical source entries, 38 controlled claim records, eight comparison cases, 12 Cardano or Cardano-adjacent cases, 12 scored use cases and 33 qualifying field records across six stakeholder segments.

The study kept four evidence types separate: documented public facts, respondent evidence, analytical inference and IEI or TKNFRA judgement. Provider claims were not treated as independent validation. Activity was separated from adoption. Respondent evidence was synthesised at segment level; identities, raw notes and exact attribution mappings are not published.

The field sample is purposive, not statistically representative. Its value is in testing objections, operating requirements, alternatives and decision criteria. It cannot support population-prevalence claims.

## Slide 4 — Market finding

The market for agent payments and agent commerce is real. Open protocols and commercial initiatives now address agent communication, authorization, purchasing and machine payments. This is no longer only a speculative category.

However, the need for a public blockchain is selective. In closed enterprise or consumer workflows, conventional payment, identity, authorization and marketplace controls can often solve the problem with lower integration and operational burden.

The strongest blockchain case appears when independent organisations need programmable settlement, conditional release, refunds, a neutral transaction record or a shared minimal receipt. Even there, the blockchain component must be compared against marketplace escrow, payment facilitators, signed logs, credentials and conventional databases.

The decision implication is narrow: Cardano should target cross-organisation failure modes that buyers already recognise. It should not lead with a broad AI narrative or assume that autonomous agents create a blockchain requirement by themselves.

## Slide 5 — Cardano activity evidence

Masumi and Sokosumi are the strongest current Cardano proof point. Masumi combines an agent registry, conditional payment, refunds and service records in an operating Cardano implementation. Sokosumi turns that infrastructure into priced services and an observable marketplace workflow.

The final refreshed evidence includes three activity measures. The Cardano app profile showed 1,852 Masumi transactions in the 30 days captured by its 21 October snapshot. Masumi reported more than 40,000 cumulative on-chain transactions. The Sokosumi Serviceplan page displayed 4,229 tasks on 29 October, while labelling the profile data 27 September.

These measures are not directly comparable. They use different periods, systems and activity definitions, and some are provider-reported. They establish live capability and observable activity. They do not establish unique external buyers, paid value, repeat use, accepted outcomes or product-market fit.

The correct interpretation is therefore that Cardano has a working reference implementation worth testing, not that the commercial case is already proven.

## Slide 6 — Competitive position

Cardano's strongest competitive feature is the coherence of the Masumi implementation: registry, escrow, refund and service evidence are combined in one operational stack. Cardano's predictable execution model and programmable controls can also support bounded settlement rules.

Its decisive weakness is distribution. Agent protocols, wallets, stablecoins, payment facilitators and merchant channels reach more developers and buyers through other ecosystems. Field evidence repeatedly identified distribution and integration effort as more decisive than feature completeness.

The practical strategy is to integrate with open standards, not create another proprietary agent stack. Agent communication and tool access are already served by open protocols. Authorization and identity should be interoperable complements. Payment execution should use existing channels where possible. Cardano's candidate role is the conditional settlement or evidence function that sits behind those interfaces.

Signed logs, credentials, conventional databases and marketplace escrow remain credible alternatives. Any Cardano test must compare itself against those alternatives on buyer value, integration effort and operational outcomes.

## Slide 7 — Priority use cases

The highest-ranked use case is escrowed purchase of machine-delivered services, with a weighted score of 3.65. Its value proposition is conditional release, refund and service evidence where non-delivery or dispute risk is material. Its next proof requirement is a paid workflow defined with two providers and at least three external buyers.

Conditional API or data access ranks second at 3.55. It should advance only if a buyer prefers conditional settlement over a facilitator, subscription or conventional payment route.

Minimal agent-action and payment receipts rank third at 3.50. This is not a claim that all AI actions belong on-chain. The test is whether an independent risk or audit owner accepts a minimal, privacy-aware receipt as useful for a named control or dispute decision.

Payment-linked identity is supporting infrastructure rather than a separate strategic category. Compute markets, model markets and generic AI applications do not pass the Cardano-specific test and should not receive vertical funding under this thesis.

## Slide 8 — Audience credibility

Different audiences require different proof. Builders and agent platforms need low integration effort, portable authorization, recovery behaviour and clear failure modes. Payment and wallet firms need a stablecoin route, reconciliation, refunds and accountable support ownership.

Institutional risk and audit owners need a minimal receipt schema, privacy boundaries, retention rules, an accountable operator and a clear statement of which control or dispute decision the evidence supports. Cardano stakeholders need independent paying buyers, repeat production use and measurable completion or dispute outcomes.

This means a single Cardano narrative will not convert the market. Claims should be specific to a use case and audience. Unsupported claims should be retired early. Credibility increases when alternatives, uncertainty and stop conditions remain visible.

## Slide 9 — Conditional-go decision

The recommendation advances through four conditions.

First, independent demand: named external buyers must define a paid workflow and a reason to prefer conditional settlement.

Second, open interoperability: the design should integrate with existing agent, authorization, wallet, payment and stablecoin infrastructure. A proprietary Cardano-only standard would deepen the distribution disadvantage.

Third, production outcomes: completion, refund, repeat use, dispute and support outcomes must become observable. Transaction counts alone are not enough.

Fourth, Cardano dependency: the evidence must show that the Cardano-based control changes a purchase, risk or dispute outcome. Technical compatibility by itself does not establish this.

Funding should stop if buyers prefer simpler alternatives, if integration remains materially harder, or if the same outcome can be achieved more simply without Cardano.

## Slide 10 — Research-to-action pathway

Three evidence-building engagements are justified before implementation funding.

The first is a buyer-backed escrowed-service design. Two independent providers and at least three external buyers should define one paid workflow, acceptance criteria and the reason conditional settlement is preferable. The output is buyer commitment and a measurable outcome definition, not a product build.

The second is an open-standard integration and conformance path. A non-Cardano builder should document agent, authorization, payment, wallet and stablecoin integration, including failure recovery and support responsibility. The output is measured integration effort and an operating boundary.

The third is a minimal receipt and dispute review. Two independent risk or audit owners should assess a privacy-aware receipt schema and state which control, audit or dispute decision it would support. The output is an accepted control purpose, an accountable owner and a clear statement of what the receipt cannot prove.

After these engagements, the Product Committee can make a stronger go, conditional-go, monitor or no-go decision.

## Slide 11 — Limitations and claims to retire

The field sample is purposive and contains different interaction formats. Only five records came from institutional buyers. Public activity metrics are dynamic and partly provider-reported. The study did not perform a deep deployment, smart-contract or enterprise-control audit. It did not produce a revenue forecast, market-share estimate or six-month monitoring series.

These limits define what the evidence can support. They do not weaken the value of the negative findings.

Claims that should be retired include that Cardano makes AI safe or verified, that it leads machine payments, that transaction counts establish product-market fit, or that formal methods prove an AI model's intent or output quality. The evidence also does not support a proprietary agent standard or a broad Cardano vertical in compute, models or generic AI applications.

The defensible statement is more precise: Cardano may provide a useful conditional-settlement and minimal-evidence function for selected open agent-service transactions, provided buyers and delivery partners validate it.

## Slide 12 — Cross-RFP handoffs and next decision

Several important questions belong to adjacent Product Research workstreams. Stablecoin and payment research should address liquidity, reconciliation, refunds and billing. Privacy and Midnight work should assess selective disclosure and evidence design. Identity and authorization work should cover open credentials, delegated authority and revocation.

L2 and interoperability research should address latency, fee predictability and access through open standards. Enterprise and RWA work should identify control owners, liability and dispute requirements. Delivery-partner work should establish access to external buyers, providers and operating support.

These are handoffs, not hidden scope expansion. RFP 09 has established the strategic boundary and the evidence tests. The next Cardano investment decision should occur after buyer and integration evidence is available and before any product or pilot grant.

The final decision is therefore conditional but actionable: test one narrow assurance and conditional-settlement position, through open infrastructure, with independent buyers and explicit stop conditions. If the evidence does not meet those conditions, move the thesis to monitor or no-go rather than expanding the narrative.
