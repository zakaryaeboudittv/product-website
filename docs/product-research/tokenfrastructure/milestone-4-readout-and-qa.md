# Readout and Q&A pack

## Session purpose

The readout gives the Product Committee and Cardano community a concise account of the research question, method, evidence, findings, recommendation, limitations and next decision. The planned speaking time is 24 minutes, leaving at least 6 minutes for questions within the contracted 30-minute session.

## Speaker notes

### Slide 1 Project and final decision

State the recommendation immediately. Cardano has a credible narrow position in assurance and conditional settlement for open AI-agent services. The evidence does not support a general AI-chain or machine-payment leadership position.

### Slide 2 Research question and scope

Explain that the study began with a commercial test. It asked where an identifiable buyer, delivery route and Cardano-side value path exist. Product development, pilot execution and the optional proposal add-ons remained outside scope.

### Slide 3 Method and evidence base

Describe the screening-first method and evidence controls. Public sources, field evidence, analytical inference and vendor judgment stayed separate. Field records informed qualitative convergence and do not support population prevalence claims.

### Slide 4 Market finding

Explain that machine payments and agent commerce have reached production infrastructure. Public blockchains add the clearest value where independent parties need programmable settlement, conditional release or a neutral shared receipt. Closed enterprise and consumer workflows often have stronger non-chain alternatives.

### Slide 5 Cardano activity evidence

Present the refreshed facts carefully. The Cardano app profile showed 1,852 Masumi transactions in the 30 days captured by its 21 October snapshot. Masumi reported more than 40,000 cumulative on-chain transactions. The Sokosumi Serviceplan page displayed 4,229 tasks on 29 October while labelling the profile data 27 September. These measures establish live capability and activity. They do not establish unique buyers, paid value, repeat use or accepted outcomes.

### Slide 6 Competitive position

Explain Cardano's strongest and weakest layers. Masumi combines registry, escrow, refunds and service records in a coherent Cardano implementation. Base, Solana, Tempo, Stripe and card networks have stronger distribution. Signed logs, credentials and conventional marketplaces remain credible alternatives.

### Slide 7 Priority use cases

Present the top three use cases in order: escrowed agent-service purchase, conditional API or data access and minimal agent-action or payment receipts. Identity works as a supporting feature. Compute, model markets and generic AI applications do not pass the Cardano-specific test.

### Slide 8 Audience credibility

Explain that each audience requires different proof. Builders need integration effort and recovery evidence. Payment firms need wallet, stablecoin, reconciliation and refund support. Risk owners need a minimal receipt schema, privacy design and accountable operator. Cardano stakeholders need evidence of external paying buyers and repeat production use.

### Slide 9 Conditional-go decision

Set out four conditions: independent buyer demand, open-standard interoperability, usable distribution and evidence that the Cardano-based control changes a purchase, risk or dispute outcome. Funding should stop if those conditions do not materialise.

### Slide 10 Research-to-action pathway

Describe the three recommended engagements. First, define one paid escrowed service workflow with external buyers and providers. Second, document an open-standard integration path through payment, wallet and authorization infrastructure. Third, test a minimal receipt and dispute design with independent risk or audit owners.

### Slide 11 Limitations and claims to retire

State that the sample is purposive, dynamic metrics are provider-reported and the study did not perform a deep deployment audit. Retire broad claims that Cardano makes AI safe, leads machine payments or has proven a strategic AI vertical from transaction counts alone.

### Slide 12 Cross-RFP handoffs and next decision

Route stablecoin and wallet distribution, Midnight privacy, identity interoperability, L2 needs and delivery-partner development to adjacent workstreams. The next decision should occur after buyer and integration evidence is available, before a product or pilot grant.

## Anticipated questions

| Question | Response |
|---|---|
| Why is the recommendation conditional rather than go? | Live capability exists, but independent buyer conversion, repeat paid use and distribution remain unproven. |
| Why does Cardano need a blockchain role at all? | The role is credible only where independent parties need conditional release or a neutral shared receipt. Many closed workflows do not need it. |
| Does Masumi prove product-market fit? | It proves an operating implementation and observable activity. The public metrics do not isolate external buyers, paid value, repeat use or outcome quality. |
| Is formal verification the main differentiator? | It can strengthen a specified escrow or mandate control. It cannot prove an AI model's intent, output quality or business truth. |
| Should Cardano build its own agent standard? | No. The market rewards open and rail-agnostic standards. Cardano should provide a compatible settlement and evidence function. |
| Which use case should be tested first? | Escrowed machine-delivered services where non-delivery, refund or dispute risk is material and measurable. |
| What would reverse the recommendation? | The position should move toward no-go if buyers prefer simple payment or conventional escrow, integration remains materially harder, or Cardano-dependent outcomes cannot be shown. |
| What would justify stronger investment? | Independent paying buyers, repeat production use, more than one provider, accepted outcome and dispute evidence, and integration through existing wallets and standards. |
